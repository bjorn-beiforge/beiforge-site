The problem

Every team has a dashboard. Nobody sees the whole flow.

Large organizations have more information than ever, spread across backlogs, roadmaps, spreadsheets, slide decks, and chat. Each tool shows one slice of the work.

So when leadership asks why something is slow, the answer depends on who you ask. Value streams get redrawn on an org chart, but the work doesn't always follow. Transformation spend is hard to justify when you can't show what changed.

"Why is this slow?"

Competing answers, each true from where that person sits.

Hidden handoffs

Work waits between teams and systems, and nobody owns the wait.

Invisible steps

Parts of the flow have no system of record and no one watching.

Stale pictures

Decisions get made from last quarter's slides.

The idea

The gap is the signal.

Every organization has two pictures of how it creates value: the one it intends, and the one that actually happens. SignalForge puts them side by side. The difference between them is where the real work is.

02

03

04

Work

Delivery data from Jira and Azure DevOps, connected to the steps it belongs to. In development

Many perspectives, on purpose

Participants rate each step and vote on where friction is. When groups describe the same step differently, SignalForge shows it. That disagreement is information, not noise.

Metrics defined once

A metric is defined once and referenced wherever it applies, so the same number means the same thing in every view.

Our principle

Context doesn't provide decisions.
Context provides discernment.

SignalForge doesn't tell you what to do. It shows you what's connected, where perspectives differ, and what changed, so the people accountable can decide well. Any insight it offers comes with its evidence, so you can always ask: why are you showing me this?

The pilot

90 days to show value.

The cost of an experiment should be small. The cost of not knowing usually isn't.

One value stream, one fixed price, mapped and running in your Microsoft 365. You define what success looks like before we start, and you keep everything at the end.

  1. Weeks 1–2

    Picture day 90

    Install Light in your SharePoint. Your sponsor and team write down what they want to be able to see and do by day 90, and pick the measures that will show it.

  2. Weeks 3–4

    Map the stream

    A facilitated Value Stream Identification workshop, working through process, then people, then systems. Your leaders confirm the map reflects reality.

  3. Weeks 5–10

    Surface the signals

    The people in the stream add their view. Short check-ins every two weeks review friction, disagreement, and blind spots.

  4. Weeks 11–13

    Read out honestly

    We measure against the picture you wrote in week one, and give you a straight recommendation on whether to continue.

What you'll measure

Signals you can see in 90 days.

Lead time and flow efficiency matter, but they take months to move. The pilot tracks leading signals that show change has started. You pick the three that matter most to you.

  • Friction-to-actionHow many of the top friction points have an owner and a next step
  • Perspective divergenceWhere groups describe the same work differently
  • Time to answerHow long a key leadership question takes to answer, before and after
  • Friction hotspotsWhich steps the people doing the work find hardest
  • Wait statesWhere work sits waiting between steps
  • Handoffs and dependenciesHow often work crosses between teams and systems
  • Visibility gapsSteps with no system of record or no one watching
  • Decisions informedDecisions you made or changed because of what the map showed
